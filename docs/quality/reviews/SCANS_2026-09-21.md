# The two mechanical scans (2026-09-21)

Section 26 produced five findings, and three of them were **the same
sentence**: *a failure that looks like an empty result.* One sentence found
three times by hand is a sentence worth looking for by machine. Two scans came
out of that, and this is what they found — and, just as important, what they
cannot find.

---

## Scan 1 — every handler that answers a failure with a falsy value

**The question:** which `except` blocks `return None` / `return False` /
`return []` / `return {}` / `return ''`?

**33 hits.** Most are harmless, and for a reason worth writing down: the
caller **refuses** on a falsy answer, and nothing is lost by refusing. A
falsy answer is only dangerous where falsy means *go ahead*.

One finding: **E6** — `_get_disallowed_action_reason` answered "no reason to
refuse" when the container configuration could not be read, so a scheduled
action touched a container without confirming it was still allowed. Recorded
as SPEC.md **B14**, fixed in `2a156f4`.

1 of 33 is not a poor yield. The other 32 are now *known* to be harmless
rather than unexamined, which is the difference the programme exists to make.

---

## Scan 2 — handlers that cannot catch a DDC exception

**The question:** `services/exceptions.py` defines `DDCBaseException(Exception)`
with some fifty subclasses. Where does a narrow `except (...)` tuple sit in
front of a call that raises one of them?

This scan came from a pattern the review kept meeting: **a repair that made
one thing safe made another thing fragile.** Review D1 gave
`_heal_if_lagging` a `raise` so that a mech snapshot lagging behind the event
log could no longer quietly bury a donation. Correct — and every handler
written before D1 that calls into the mech now has a hole in it, on paths
nobody re-read.

**11 hits, reduced to 9 after two were judged duplicates. All nine judged:**

### The one that mattered: E12

The monthly donation task grants the mech $1.00 when its power has run to
zero, and the handler around that grant says, in a comment,
*"Continue anyway to send the message"* — exactly right, because the appeal has
nothing to do with whether the gift worked. But the tuple was
`(ValueError, TypeError, RuntimeError)`, and `add_system_donation` calls
`_heal_if_lagging`. `MechStateError` was in **none of the three tuples in that
file**, so it left the task entirely and the month's appeal was sent to
nobody — for a reason that had nothing to do with the appeal.

Second location of the same sentence as **E8**. That is what a scan is for:
by hand, E8 was found and the search stopped there.

### The one the scan got wrong: #1

`save_spam_protection` calls `spam_service.save_config(config)`. That is
`SpamProtectionService.save_config`, which returns a `ServiceResult` and
raises nothing of the kind — a **different method that happens to share a
name** with the config service's.

**The scan matches by name, and this is its limit.** It cannot tell two
`save_config` methods apart, and every hit therefore has to be read back to
the definition before it counts. One in nine was noise here; a bigger scan
would produce more. Recorded so the next person running it does not take the
hit list for a finding list.

### The one that was already right: #8

`_save_configuration_files` calls the legacy `save_config` wrapper, and
`ConfigService.save_config` **raises** `ConfigSaveError` rather than returning
`success=False`. That exception does escape the three handlers in that
method — and lands one level up in `save_configuration`'s
`except ConfigServiceError`, whose comment already names `save_config` as its
source. The operator gets an honest "Error saving configuration: …".

Worth stating plainly: **the scan hit is real and the code is correct.** A
handler being narrow is not a defect when the right handler is one frame up.

### Noted while judging #8, not repaired

`_save_server_order` (step 5) writes the server order file *before* the main
configuration is saved in step 6. If step 6 fails, the order file has already
been changed and the panel reports a failed save. A partial write, pre-existing
and unrelated to this scan. Not pursued.

## What the two scans say about the programme

Both scans came from the same observation — a sentence found three times by
hand — and both paid for themselves on the *second* instance of a sentence the
review had already found once. The technique is not "read everything again";
it is **take one confirmed finding and ask the machine where else it lives.**

The cost was under an hour each. The limit is that both are text scans: they
find shapes, not behaviour, and every hit has to be read back to the
definition before it counts as anything.
